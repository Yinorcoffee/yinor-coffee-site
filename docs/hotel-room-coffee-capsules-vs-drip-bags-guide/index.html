Hotel Room Coffee: Capsules or Drip Bags?

September 1, 2026 · Yinor Coffee

In-room coffee is one of the few hotel touchpoints guests experience daily - and one of the most complained about when it is bad. For hotel procurement teams and hospitality suppliers, the format decision matters: capsules, drip bags, or both. Here is a practical comparison.

Drip bags: no equipment, maximum flexibility

Drip bag coffee needs nothing but a cup and hot water - which makes it ideal for hotels that do not want to install and maintain machines in every room. Drip bags are lightweight, take almost no storage space, and deliver a clean pour-over cup that reads as "specialty" to guests. They are also easy to swap flavors seasonally without changing hardware.

Capsules: consistency for machine-equipped rooms

If a hotel already runs capsule machines - or wants a premium espresso option - aluminum capsules compatible with popular machines deliver a rich cup with crema at the touch of a button. Capsules shine when consistency across thousands of rooms matters more than flexibility.

Cost and logistics at hotel scale

At property scale, both formats are economical, but the economics differ. Drip bags have a lower per-unit cost and no machine maintenance burden. Capsules add the cost and care of machines, but command a higher perceived value per cup in premium segments. Storage is a factor too: drip bags are far lighter per serving than aluminum capsules.

The guest experience angle

Guests increasingly expect good coffee, not instant. A drip bag with a branded note and a small kettle reads as thoughtful and premium. Capsules read as fast and familiar - especially for guests who use the same system at home. Many four- and five-star properties pair a capsule machine with branded drip bags as a backup, covering both expectations.

Private label makes either format yours

The smartest move for hotel groups is branding. Both drip bags and capsules can carry your logo - from the pack itself to the outer box and the note card left on the tray. Private label and OEM programs let a hotel chain build a consistent coffee experience across properties without owning a roastery or factory.

A simple rule of thumb

No machines in rooms? Start with drip bags. Machines already installed, or targeting a premium espresso moment? Add capsules. Most growing hotel programs end up running both - drip bags as the flexible default, capsules as the premium option.
